# Copy rules

One file. Universal. Brands may add constraints. They may not relax these.

Load this before writing, reviewing, or revising. Scan the lists. Then write. After the draft, run the audit at the bottom.

Tone: a strategic guide talking to one partner, not a guru performing on a stage. If the words feel automated, the reader assumes the thinking is too.

Regional English comes from the brand `context/style-guide.md`, else `_canon/canon-default-voice.md`.

---

## Words

### Banned vocabulary

delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, realm, embark, unlock, elevate, leverage, robust, seamless, navigate, harness, foster, pivotal, crucial, essential, vibrant, ever-evolving, game-changer, journey, ecosystem, synergy, empower, solutions (as a product noun)

### No intensifiers

truly, genuinely, incredibly, deeply, remarkably, very, really

### No formal transitions

Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, Ultimately, That being said, In conclusion.

Use "And," "But," "So," or nothing.

### No filler phrases

- It's important to note
- Remember
- At the end of the day
- The bottom line is
- In today's fast-paced world
- Here's the thing / here's the reality / here's why this matters
- Let me explain / let me break this down
- The truth is
- Here's why / here's how (also banned in headlines)

### No vague attribution

Banned: "studies show," "experts agree," "many people find," "research proves," "scientists have found."

Name the source or cut the claim.

## Sentences

1. **No em dashes.** Zero. Never use —. Use a comma, period, colon, semicolon, or rewrite.

2. **No three parallel clauses with matching structure.** Repeating one word or phrase for emphasis is fine. Stacking "A designer creates, an engineer specifies, a builder quotes" is not.

3. **Contrast must carry weight.** "It's not X, it's Y" / "Don't X. Do Y" / "Amateurs X. Pros Y" is allowed only when Y introduces a mechanism, trade-off, or decision. Maximum two per piece. Swap the nouns. If it still sounds profound, it is fluff.

4. **Questions are fine when they are genuine.** Never ask one and answer it in the next sentence. Never "Sound familiar?" Never "Why does X matter? It matters because..."

5. **No -ing tail phrases.** Cut "…ensuring," "…highlighting the importance of," "…showcasing," "…underscoring," "…reflecting."

6. **No hedging.** Cut "can potentially," "may help," "some might find," "it's possible that." State the position.

7. **No fragment stacks.** Short sentences land a thought. They do not pace a thriller. Two or more sentences in a row under five words is a fail.

8. **Never open with** "Imagine," "Picture this," or "We get it."

---

## Structure

1. **First sentence does work.** Open with a story, a real scenario, or a concrete observation. Never restate the headline. Never preview the article.

2. **Every section has one concrete anchor:** a real situation, a number, or a name. Every reasoning claim has a mechanism (why this produces that).

3. **Stating the conventional view is fine** when the next move reframes it. Defining things the reader already knows is not.

4. **Headers only where the topic changes.** Sentence case. Capitalize the first word and proper nouns only.

5. **Prose by default.** Bullets only for things that are actually a list.

6. **Bold nothing** unless the reader is scanning for that exact item.

7. **No "Key takeaways."** No bolted-on FAQ. No emoji bullets. No summary conclusion. End on the last useful point.

8. **One principle statement per story, in plain language.** If the last sentence of a paragraph would work on a coffee mug, rewrite it.

9. **Banned headline templates:** "Ultimate guide," "Everything you need to know," "X: what nobody tells you," "Here's why," "Here's how."

---

## Voice

1. **First person, mostly "we,"** standing alongside the reader. The writer is the brand. The reader is "you." Never "the company manages" or "business owners need to." Personal anecdote sparingly and without self-promotion.

2. **Take a clear position.** Critique the framework, never the person holding it. Grant good intentions.

3. **Don't flatter the reader.** Don't manufacture empathy. Don't inflate stakes with "critical" or "must-have." Show the consequence instead.

4. **Contractions are fine.** Starting with "And" is fine. Perfect grammar with no voice is not.

---

## Length

Write until the point is made, then stop. No word-count targets. Last pass: cut any paragraph that costs nothing to lose.

## Brocabulary patterns

These six patterns are the guru tells. Flag and rewrite. Context over contrast. Bridges over punchlines. If you would not say it on a phone call to a partner you respect, cut it or fold it into a longer thought.

### 1. Binary trap

Wrong way vs right way with no middle.

| Fail | Pass |
|---|---|
| Stop chasing leads. Start building systems. | Chasing leads can fill a week. A system that brings the right people to you usually holds up longer, because you are not starting from zero every Monday. |

Fix: explain the trade-off and the why. 90% of antithesis is fluff.

### 2. Staccato fatigue

Short. Short. Fragment. Short. No "and," "but," or "because."

| Fail | Pass |
|---|---|
| Set the goal. Do the work. Win the day. | Once the goal is set, the hard part is showing up every day to do the work that actually moves it. |

Fix: connect ideas with because / so / which means.

### 3. Wisdom mirror

A-B, B-A. Fortune-cookie flip.

| Fail | Pass |
|---|---|
| Don't let your business run you; you run your business. | Growing usually costs comfort. That is the trade-off if you want the next stage. |

Fix: state the observation in one direction.

### 4. Forced three

Every list, benefit, or ending lands in exactly three parts.

| Fail | Pass |
|---|---|
| Efficient, scalable, and powerful. | Clients stick with it because the routine parts stop being reinvented every week. |

Fix: keep the one point that matters. A subhead that is specific positioning can stay. Generic triplets cannot.

### 5. Em dash addiction

Dash used as a logic shortcut or dramatic mic drop.

| Fail | Pass |
|---|---|
| Growth isn't a goal — it's a requirement. | Growth is not only a goal. You need it if you want to stay in the market you are in. |

Fix: write the connection. Zero em dashes, not "two per hundred words."

### 6. Leading question

Ask, then answer in the next sentence.

| Fail | Pass |
|---|---|
| What's the real problem? The problem is your mindset. | Most of the issues we see start with how the market is being read. |

Fix: state the insight. A real question the reader might type into search can be a header. A setup question cannot.

## Audit

Check these first. If they fail, the rest of the piece is probably failing too.

**Header hook.** Binary or antithesis headlines. Change to a specific result or a situational observation.

**Paragraph bookend.** Last sentence is a punchy slogan. Delete it. If the paragraph was doing its job, the point is already there.

**Transition gap.** Paragraph ends on a leading question, next paragraph answers it. Bridge with this means / because of this.

**Fail grades (any one fails the section):**

- Two or more sentences in a row under five words
- Amateur vs Pro or Don't/Do with no two sentences of why
- Any em dash
- A coffee-mug last sentence
- A binary with no mechanism
- A question answered in the next sentence

**Phone-call test.** Read it as a 60-second voice memo to a client you respect. If you cringe, it is a wisdom mirror. If you gasp between periods, it is staccato. If it is a slogan with no how, it is filler.

## Brand safety

Still law. Not optional.

1. **Never frame the brand's own products as negatives.** Differences are fit, not flaws.
2. **Never put the reader's existing decisions in a negative light.** They may have approved that choice. Assessment and opportunity, not blame.
3. **Do not claim planned, unmerged, or conflicted product as live.** Follow that brand's `wiki/claims.md` and `wiki/product.md`.
4. **Do not invent quotes, customers, or metrics.** Unsourced facts get `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. Missing input gets `[NEEDS INPUT]`.
